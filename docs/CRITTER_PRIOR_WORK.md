# Borrow Spore's primitives, keep physics offline

The prior work most useful to critters is not physics simulation or text-to-motion AI. It is a small set of kinematic primitives from games and graphics, mainly Spore's gait and retargeting system, follow-the-leader spines and Tu and Terzopoulos's artificial fish. Two sources supply the numbers: biomechanics (Hildebrand's duty factor and phase, Alexander's Froude scaling, fish wave envelopes) and frame-level measurement of footage. MuJoCo belongs nowhere in the runtime. Its official WebAssembly build is about **2.5 MB gzipped, roughly 37 times the whole 70 KB runtime**. Even the fruit-fly model needed real footage and about 10^9 training steps to move believably. A few hundred lines of hand-written TypeScript geometry in the existing `npm run qa:*` harness covers nearly every offline check critters needs. Three current numbers fail the evidence bar. The robin's 10–15 wingbeats/s has no source, and small passerines bound with their wings folded rather than gliding. The frog's 190/75/160 ms crouch and 52 ms leg offset have no source, and the repo itself says no footage log exists yet. Froude ≈ 0.5 is a sourced transition for biped walk-to-run and crow walk-to-hop, not for quadruped gait changes. Nothing found combines per-behaviour footage citations, contact-level acceptance checks, motive-driven behaviour and a tiny agent-built web runtime. Each piece has precedent, though, and critters should credit and borrow from it rather than claim each piece as new.

## Physics engines cost 37 times the runtime and still need footage

DeepMind's official `@mujoco/mujoco` 3.15.0 package ships a single-threaded `mujoco.wasm` of **10.4 MB raw and 2.54 MB gzipped**, plus 68 KB of gzipped JavaScript glue (measured from the npm tarball, [npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/@mujoco/mujoco)). Its README still calls the bindings "a WIP" and "not yet complete". C++-backed objects have to be freed by hand with `.delete()`. The multi-threaded build fails to start unless the host page sends `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy` and `Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy` headers ([MuJoCo wasm README](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/tree/main/wasm)). For a drop-in web component, each of these is disqualifying on its own. General 2D engines are lighter but still out of proportion. **matter-js is 26 KB gzipped, planck 55 KB and box2d-wasm about 114 KB** ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/), measured locally), so any one of them would take a third or more of the runtime budget. They would bring broadphase, contact solving and joint types that a creature with kinematically pinned feet never uses.

The stronger argument is about what physics actually delivers. **No MuJoCo model exists for a frog, beetle, koi, robin or snake.** Menagerie's only animal is the fruit fly ([MuJoCo Menagerie](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_menagerie)), and dm_control's swimmer and fish are abstract chains ([dm_control suite](https://github.com/google-deepmind/dm_control/tree/main/dm_control/suite)). The flagship animal result, flybody, used a 102-DoF body. It imitated about 80 minutes of fly footage filmed at 150 fps and took **about 10^9 simulation steps** of distributed RL to walk. Even then its 3D swing height was "an assumed sine arc", the same kind of authored kinematic prior critters already writes by hand ([Vaxenburg et al. 2025, PMC12310536](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12310536/)). GPU stacks such as MuJoCo Playground train robot gaits in 5–30 minutes on two RTX 4090s. They can do that only because the robot model, reward and task already exist ([Playground, arXiv 2502.08844](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08844)). For a new animal, most of the cost is engineering, not GPU time. Physics turns reference motion into robust, interactive motion. It does not invent believable motion. Animators and researchers have said so for decades: "physically valid motion does not necessarily make the animation look more visually appealing" ([Shapiro and Feng, USC ICT](https://ict.usc.edu/pubs/Practical%20Character%20Physics%20For%20Animators.pdf)). Evolved physical characters "are often missing the expressive content" of drawn ones ([Ventrella](https://www.ventrella.com/Alife/Disney/disney_2.html)). The steelman for physics is robustness to perturbation, and an ambient top-down pond rarely needs that. No controlled perceptual study comparing physics-driven and kinematic animal motion was found, so this verdict rests on cost and on expert opinion, not on a measured believability gap.

Physics still has two narrow, legitimate roles. The first is offline checking. Shapiro and Feng's model of physics as "a non-destructive reference path" the animator compares against, with the animator keeping control, is the precedent. In practice the needed checks are plain geometry that runs inside the existing harness, with no engine: centre of mass inside the planted-foot hull, a parabola during flight, no acceleration without contact. The single-threaded MuJoCo build loads in Node without special headers, so a dynamic-feasibility test is possible later. Today it is only meaningful for the fly or the rodent, dog and ant proxies. The second role is small deterministic constraint chains for secondary motion such as koi tails, antennae, a frog's tongue or a snake body. Spore built its IK on exactly this kind of Jakobsen-style particle-and-length-constraint relaxation ([Hecker et al. 2008](https://chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)). The research notes estimate such a chain at 1–3 KB minified. That figure is an estimate, not a measurement.

## Spore already solved most of the kit's addressing and gait problems

Hecker et al.'s SIGGRAPH 2008 paper on Spore is the closest precedent for a general kit. It is also a primary source with full detail ([Hecker et al. 2008](https://chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)). Spore never names bones. Animation channels select body parts through **semantic "capabilities" and spatial queries** such as "all graspers", "FrontMost foot" or "Left setspace", and game AI uses the same queries. Keys are stored in **normalised movement modes**: ground-relative (0 = rest height, 1 = touching the ground) and target-relative (0 = rest, 1 = at the target). One authored peck or lunge therefore lands correctly on every body. Locomotion comes from a separate gait synthesiser. It clusters legs into groups of roughly equal length, harmonises group frequencies with **small rational ratios**, and gives each foot a **duty factor and a step-trigger offset**. Swing arcs are authored once in leg-length-normalised space. Creatures without feet get an inch-worm "pseudo-feet" crawl. Parts not driven by IK get **passive, highly damped "Jiggles" that never feed back**. An earlier version that did feed back "negatively impacted the quality of the animators' work". Spore also ran an **Animation Validation Grid**, stochastic tests of about a thousand animations against several hundred creatures, with roughly a 90% pass rate. That is a direct ancestor of critters' QA harness. Spore's lessons cut both ways. A fully general body graph "was actually a hindrance", which supports critters' spine-plus-limbs tree. And animators needed "weeks to build up an intuition about which kinds of motions generalize".

The open-source procedural lineage confirms what a kit should leave out. argonaut's widely copied fish, snake and lizard comes to about 16 KB of Processing source. Its spine is pure follow-the-leader with an angle limit, and its body outline comes from a per-vertebra width array. Both are ideal top-down primitives. Its legs use "threshold-teleport-lerp" stepping, with no coordination between legs and no swing arc, and the author calls the result "Glitchy lil dude" ([argonaut Lizard.pde](https://github.com/argonautcode/animal-proc-anim/blob/main/Lizard.pde)). The professional systems add exactly what that hobbyist pattern lacks. Johansen's semi-procedural locomotion system extracts footfall timings from example cycles and works "with any amount of legs" ([Johansen thesis](https://runevision.com/thesis/rune_skovbo_johansen_thesis.pdf)). The Spider Walker add-on predicts landing points ahead of velocity and bounds step time between a minimum and a maximum ([Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/08/28/spider-walker-automates-multi-leg-animation/)). Little Polygon advances a phase accumulator in proportion to speed and derives hip bob from that phase ([Little Polygon](https://blog.littlepolygon.com/posts/loco1/)). David Rosen's Overgrowth talk is often summarised as driving the run cycle by distance travelled with very few key poses ([GDC Vault](http://www.gdcvault.com/play/1020583/Animation-Bootcamp-An-Indie-Approach)). The specific two-pose and 13-keyframe details exist only secondhand and are **unverified**.

Classic graphics research contributes structure, not dynamics. Tu and Terzopoulos's artificial fish turns using **four tuned parameter sets for 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°, interpolated, with larger turns chained** from smaller ones. Its intention generator ranks hunger, libido and fear in a fixed priority order and keeps a one-item memory for resuming interrupted intentions ([Tu and Terzopoulos 1994](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spr15/cos426/papers/Tu94.pdf)). Miller's snakes move by a travelling wave plus directional friction ([Miller 1988](https://ohiostate.pressbooks.pub/app/uploads/sites/45/2017/09/snakesandworms2.pdf)). Kinematically, that becomes a curvature wave on a follow-the-leader spine. Coros et al. use a **dual leg frame**: front and rear girdles joined by a flexible spine ([Coros et al. 2011](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2011-TOG-quadruped/index.html)). Seen from above, that lets the rear feet step where the front feet stepped. The data-driven line (MANN, DeepPhase) needs dog motion capture and a neural network at runtime. Its one transferable idea is DeepPhase's representation. Each periodic channel is a 2D phase vector whose amplitude falls to zero at rest, so idle-to-walk transitions need no special case ([DeepPhase](https://i.cs.hku.hk/~taku/deepphase.pdf)). Rain World adds a caution. Its developers kept a coarse simulated body of a few point "chunks" and drew rich graphics on top. Even so, they wrote most of its 100-plus creatures "from scratch" with unique movement code ([Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/exploring-procedural-design-rain-world)). Critters should expect a locomotion driver per body plan on top of shared primitives, not one universal controller.

The current kit plan (`docs/CRITTER_KIT_PLAN.md`) has no module for a gait timeline, a spine chain, a body wave or passive secondary motion. These are the gaps the prior work fills:

## Biology supplies gait parameters, but three critters numbers lack a source

Hildebrand's framework turns any symmetrical gait into two numbers. **Duty factor** is the share of the stride a foot is on the ground. **Phase** is the share by which the forefoot's footfall lags the hindfoot's on the same side. Walks have a duty factor above 0.5. Phases below 50% give lateral-sequence gaits, and phases above 50% give diagonal-sequence gaits ([Wikipedia, Gait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gait); [Cartmill et al. 2002](https://www.originalwisdom.com/wp-content/uploads/bsk-pdf-manager/2019/10/Cartmill-et-al_2002_Support-polygons-and-symmetrical-gaits-in-mammals.pdf)). In practice animals use few combinations. Usherwood and Self Davies's video survey found a **"slow" group of reptiles, amphibians, mice and hippos** with a high duty factor and **phase ≈ 40–50%**, and a "normal mammal" group with phase ≈ 25% ([eLife 29495](https://elifesciences.org/articles/29495/figures)). Its editors first asked for lab corroboration of data taken from YouTube, so treat its clusters as qualitative ([eLife reviews](https://elifesciences.org/articles/29495/peer-reviews)). Robotics papers state the same model directly: a lateral-sequence walk with D = 0.75 and Φ = 0.25, a trot with D = 0.5 and Φ = 0.5 ([arXiv 2112.00662](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.00662)). This matters for critters. The spec's single "quadruped walk" row lists lizards alongside cats and dogs. The evidence puts lizards in the slow group, with a phase nearer 0.4–0.5, so the row should split.

Speed scaling comes from Alexander. The Froude number is Fr = v²/(g·h), with h the hip height. Dynamically similar animals share relative stride length at equal Fr, and **λ/h ≈ 2.3·Fr^0.3** ([SJSU summary of Alexander 1976](https://www.sjsu.edu/faculty/watkins/alexander.htm); [Acta Palaeontologica Polonica](https://www.app.pan.pl/archive/published/app46/app46-235.pdf)). That gives a size-consistent recipe: choose Fr, then derive speed, stride length and stride period. Insects follow a continuum from wave to tetrapod to tripod gaits as speed rises. _Drosophila_ changes speed "almost exclusively via step frequency" ([Wosnitza et al. 2013](https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article-split/216/3/480/11966/Inter-leg-coordination-in-the-control-of-walking)). Cockroaches tripod from 0.44 to 1.0 m/s, and stride frequency approaches 27 Hz near their top speed ([Full and Tu 1991](https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article-pdf/156/1/215/2579388/jexbio_156_1_215.pdf)). The beetle's tripod duty factor of 0.5 (0.6 when slow) matches the geometric rule β = 1 − (legs swinging)/6.

Carangiform fish such as koi have a lateral amplitude near zero over the front fifth of the body. It **rises to about 0.10 L at the tail, with a wavelength of about 1.05 L** ([arXiv 1812.02410](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.02410); [ar5iv 2010.04748](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2010.04748)). Bainbridge's rule U = L(0.75f − 1) applies only above 5 Hz, so a koi cruising slowly is an extrapolation from it ([USGS summary](https://www.umesc.usgs.gov/data_library/fisheries/fish_passage/bainbridge.html)). A C-start's first stage takes about 11–18 ms ([Jayne and Lauder 1993](https://sites.harvard.edu/glauder/files/2022/03/JayneLauder1993.pdf)), which is about one frame at 60 fps. Frogs swim with a **synchronous two-leg kick**, and alternating kicks give "significantly lower velocity" ([Nauwelaerts and Aerts, SICB](https://sicb.org/?p=37442)). Toads land on their forelimbs, aligning them with the velocity vector well before touchdown ([Cox and Gillis 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5278434)). Which snake mode is used depends on the substrate, not speed ([Jayne 1986 reference list](https://userweb.ucs.louisiana.edu/%7Ebrm2286/locrefs.htm)).

## Video tools give ratios cheaply; absolute timings need a known capture rate

Footage cannot answer "how long?" without knowing how fast it was captured. Container frame rate is not capture frame rate. Ratios such as duty factor and phase offset survive an unknown slow-motion factor, but absolute periods do not. Video-gait studies concluded that "a minimum video sampling rate of 100 Hz is required to detect key events" ([University of Bath](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/automating-video-based-two-dimensional-motion-analysis-in-sport-i/)). At 30 fps, a 75 ms event spans about two frames and its measured duration can be off by up to 44%. The footage log should therefore record capture rate and slow-motion factor with their source. The working rule is at least 120 fps for events around 75 ms, and 240 fps for insect stepping and wingbeats.

DeepLabCut 3.0.x is the cheapest automated first pass. `video_inference_superanimal()` runs SuperAnimal-Quadruped (39 keypoints, mostly side view) or TopViewMouse with no labelling ([DLC Model Zoo](https://deeplabcut.github.io/DeepLabCut/docs/ModelZoo.html)). It has three catches. Its model card says it is not suited to zero-shot use "where high keypoint precision is required". The weights are **non-commercial, research use only**. And no bird, fish, frog or insect model exists ([Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/mwmathis/DeepLabCutModelZoo-SuperAnimal-Quadruped)). For every critters species, the realistic route is one of two. The first is labelling 30–100 frames in DLC or SLEAP and training a single-clip model ([SLEAP 1.5](https://docs.sleap.ai/v1.5.1/guides/migrating-to-sleap-1-5)). The second, often better for small animals, is extracting frames with `ffmpeg` and marking touchdown and liftoff by eye on contact sheets. No turnkey footfall detector for in-the-wild 2D keypoints exists, so practitioners threshold paw speed against the ground. One horse study detected 92% of strides that way from DLC landmarks ([Auburn thesis](https://etd.auburn.edu/handle/10415/7894)).

Monocular 3D reconstruction is for pose reference, not timing. Kirin (ECCV 2026), the newest in-the-wild animal pipeline, admits to "foot sliding or floating", applies no contact constraint and reports no foot-contact metric ([Kirin, arXiv 2609.01823](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01823)). Licensing deserves a line in the skill. YouTube's Terms forbid downloading content "except as expressly authorized" ([University of Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/copyright-at-waterloo/node/198)). The defensible workflow is clips the user supplies or that carry a Creative Commons licence, with the log storing measurements, URLs and time ranges rather than redistributing frames. That last point is a reasoned inference, not legal advice.

## Text-to-animal-motion models generate clips, not cited and checked behaviour

The AI neighbours are busy but aimed elsewhere. OmniMotionGPT ([CVPR 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Yang_OmniMotionGPT_Animal_Motion_Generation_with_Limited_Data_CVPR_2024_paper.html)), AniMo with **78,149 sequences across 114 species** ([CVPR 2025 poster](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2025/poster/34318)), How to Move Your Dragon on the Truebones Zoo, which includes insects ([ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/lee25j.html)), QuadFM, which targets robots ([arXiv 2603.24021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24021)), and Kirin all output 3D skeletal clips. They score themselves with distribution metrics such as FID, R-Precision and diversity, not per-clip contact checks. Kirin's project page describes foot contact only qualitatively ([Kirin project page](https://kirin-ani.github.io/)). Commercial tools (Meshy, Tripo, Cascadeur's alpha quadruped support) follow a mesh, auto-rig, preset-clip pipeline ([Meshy docs](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/guides/animate); [Cascadeur 2025.3](https://cascadeur.com/blog/view/cascadeur-2025-3-new-inbetweening-quadrupeds)). Video generators have documented failures such as duplicated animals, but the evidence is anecdotal ([Promptus comparison](https://www.promptus.ai/blog/veo-3-vs-sora-ai-video-generation-comparison)). Code-generation benchmarks show LLMs are weak at physical dynamics. The best model scores 21.5% on SimuScene ([arXiv 2602.10840](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10840)). WorldCoder-Bench argues that screenshot scoring misses hidden runtime state ([arXiv 2606.01869](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01869)), which directly supports critters' choice to measure motion state rather than judge frames.

The nearest neighbours each cover part of the combination. Ryan's **Anatomy** skill is the closest in packaging: a one-line prompt produces a framework-free TypeScript interactive figure with automated geometric audits and effort sizing, but for machines, not animals ([Anatomy](https://skills.wheresryan.sh/anatomy)). **Kirin** is the closest in motion grounding, but the grounding is a learned prior over a large web-video corpus, not a named clip cited for a named parameter. The community **`threejs-procedural-creatures`** Claude Code skill claims walking, flying and swimming from a JSON spec. Its listing gives no gait details, motion checks or footage references, and the repo source was not read ([skills.rest listing](https://skills.rest/skill/threejs-procedural-creatures)). The novelty claim also needs one correction. "Every action has a motive" is not new in itself. Tu and Terzopoulos's fish had hunger, fear and libido driving behaviour in 1994, and Rain World's creatures run prey, threat and den trackers. What is new is binding motive to footage citations and to acceptance checks in a method an agent can follow.

## Critters' checks are stricter than the literature in kind but miss five measures

The motion-generation literature measures foot skating three ways. One is a **skating ratio**: the share of frames where a near-ground foot moves more than 2.5 cm while below 5 cm height ([GMD, arXiv 2305.12577](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.12577)). Another is PhysDiff's **mean horizontal displacement** of contacting feet in mm ([PhysDiff](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.02500)). The third is a **height-weighted skate**, s = v·(2 − 2^{h/H}) with H = 2.5 cm, which tolerates slow touchdown ([arXiv 2304.02061](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02061)). A survey notes there is no standardised definition ([arXiv 2307.10894](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.10894)). Critters' Q-12 and Q-13 use ground-truth pins rather than inferred contact. Nothing in the literature matches that, and it is the most defensible part of the suite. Perception data supports tight thresholds. Viewers notice footsliding **below 21 mm in most conditions** for human characters ([Pražák, Hoyet and O'Sullivan 2011](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/SCA.SCA11.287-294)). Sensitivity is higher on more human-like characters ([Reitsma, Andrews and Pollard 2008](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/CGF.v27i2pp201-210)), so human thresholds are conservative for animals. Converting 21 mm against human height suggests Q-13's 0.01 BL median is about right. Q-12's 0.06 BL p95 per stretch is probably visible on a large creature, but that conversion is an inference, since screen size and viewing distance were not reported.

Five additions would close real gaps. First, a **skating ratio over inferred contact** would catch feet near a surface that are not formally pinned, such as a dragging lift-off. Second, a **ballistic check in `air`**: constant horizontal velocity and constant vertical acceleration. Reitsma and Pollard found horizontal-velocity errors and added accelerations the most detectable errors in jumps ([CMU RI](https://ri.cmu.edu/?p=8709)). Third, **SPARC per segmented movement** (one stroke, one hop, one step). It is the smoothness measure Balasubramanian et al. recommend for robustness to noise. They warn that whole-cycle smoothness scores penalise rhythm itself, and critters' state machine provides the segments for free ([Balasubramanian et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4674971/)). Fourth, **phase-lag error and a Robinson-style symmetry index** for walkers, which extends the frog's |L−R| mirroring test to the beetle and lizard ([JURA, quantifying gait symmetry](https://journalhosting.ucalgary.ca/index.php/jura/article/view/30060)). The Robinson formula itself is **unverified** against the 1987 original. Fifth, a **static-stability test**: when slow, the centre of mass stays inside the planted-foot hull, after the Pose Stability Duration metric ([arXiv 2502.04483](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04483)). Two lower-priority ideas are worth trying. EDGE's physical-foot-contact logic penalises sliding only while the body accelerates ([EDGE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.10658)), which suits the frog's push-off. A stride-time variability floor guards against robotic gaits; healthy humans show about 1.8% coefficient of variation ([Beauchet et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16095533)), and no animal-animation threshold exists.

Two existing checks should change units. "Turning while still ≤ 0.05 rad/frame" and "largest one-frame pose change" are per-frame bounds. They change meaning if the step rate changes, so restate them per second (0.05 rad/frame at 60 fps is 3 rad/s). The literature offers critters things to avoid as well. FID scores swing from 5.9 to 80.4 for the same motion in different representations ([PhysiInter, arXiv 2506.07456](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07456)). Jitter inflates diversity scores, and a model can score better than ground truth ([EDGE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.10658)). Both are reasons to stay with state-level measurement. If critters ever wants a believability score, EDGE's protocol fits better: pairwise forced choice with control trials and binomial confidence intervals, run on before-and-after builds of one study.

## Conclusion

The prior work changes the claim critters should make about itself. It is not the first system with motive-driven creatures (Tu and Terzopoulos), automated cross-creature validation (Spore) or footage-derived animal motion (flybody, Kirin). Its novelty is the evidence chain inside one method an agent can follow: a named clip, a measured parameter, a kinematic primitive and an acceptance check that fails when the motion drifts from the evidence, all shipped in a runtime smaller than one gzipped physics engine. That chain is only as strong as its weakest number. Today the weakest numbers are the headline ones, the frog's crouch timings and the robin's wingbeat. The next unit of work should be the first real footage log, not more kit code.

There is also a design implication. Spore, Johansen and DeepPhase all split locomotion timing (phases, duty factors, triggers) from pose execution (IK, chains). Critters' biology inputs (Hildebrand β and Φ, Froude-scaled stride, wave envelopes) slot into exactly that timing layer. A `gait.ts` footfall timeline and a `chain.ts`/`wave.ts` spine should therefore be extracted before the beetle and koi are built. They are the parts where cited biology becomes measurable motion, and where the checks learn what "design" means.
