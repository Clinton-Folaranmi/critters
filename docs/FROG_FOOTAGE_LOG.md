# Frog pond — reusable movement evidence

This log is a durable evidence asset under spec P-11. The frog pond currently
does not name one biological species for all three frogs. The sources below
therefore support broad anuran movement patterns and examples; they do not
prove the pond's exact timings or make one species' joint sequence universal.

## Tree frog landing on horizontal perches

Source: Song et al. 2024, “Tree frogs (_Polypedates dennysi_) landing on
horizontal perches: the effects of perch diameter,” _Journal of Experimental
Biology_ 227, jeb246931. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.246931),
[Movie 1](https://movie.biologists.com/video/10.1242/jeb.246931/video-1).

- **Observation:** Movie 1, the full 1:13 clip, recorded with two orthogonal
  high-speed cameras at 360 fps. The clip shows _P. dennysi_ landing on
  horizontal perches of different diameters. The previously screened copy is
  described in `docs/FOOTAGE_SCREENING.md`: initial contact varies across
  trials and can be made with front feet, hind feet or the belly. The article
  also reports that contact strategies vary with perch diameter.
- **Licence / handling:** the screening record says the video licence was not
  stated. Reference its description and article; do not redistribute the clip
  or extracted frames.
- **Inference:** landing is a sequence of possible contacts selected in part
  by the target, not necessarily a four-foot simultaneous touchdown. This is
  relevant to perching frogs, but does not establish the pond frogs' exact
  landing strategy on lily pads.
- **Use in the frog pond:** qualitative context only. No timings or contact
  values from this species are copied into the pond motion sheet.

## Swimming with the hind limbs

Source: Richards 2010, “Kinematics and hydrodynamics analysis of swimming
anurans reveals striking inter-specific differences in the mechanism for
producing thrust,” _Journal of Experimental Biology_ 213, 621–632.
[Article](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.032631).

- **Evidence location:** Results, “General kinematic patterns of the joints
  and feet during swimming,” and “Species differences in the mechanism for
  producing thrust”; figures 1–8. This is a peer-reviewed kinematics study,
  not a clip measured for this repository.
- **Observation:** the study compares _Xenopus laevis_, _Hymenochirus
  boettgeri_, _Rana pipiens_ and _Bufo americanus_. It describes a power
  stroke, a glide with the legs extended, and a recovery in which the joints
  flex. It reports substantial species differences in how foot rotation and
  translation contribute to thrust, and differences in glide length.
- **Inference:** “kick and glide” is a useful family-level pattern, but
  species-specific foot motion and phase durations must not be copied
  wholesale from one frog to another.
- **Use in the frog pond:** supports the broad swimming pattern only. The
  pond's exact crouch, push, kick and recovery timings remain design values
  without a source; see spec section 15 and `docs/FROG_ART_DIRECTION.md`.

## Evidence still needed

- Identify the frog species represented by the pond, or mark the rig as a
  composite if it intentionally combines species.
- Find footage that is licensed for use and shows that species' swimming and
  landing from a comparable view; measure timing and contact only when the
  footage supports it.
- Resolve the pond's unsourced crouch, push and hind-limb delay values, or
  keep them explicitly marked as guesses.

Until those gaps are filled, this log lets new work reuse the family-level
pattern without pretending that the pond's exact timings are measured facts.
